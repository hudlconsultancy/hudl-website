## Who we are talking to

**Primary:** UK business owners and marketing directors who know their
marketing isn't working as well as it should — they're running Google Ads
but can't read the results, or they've hired agencies that talk in jargon
and deliver vanity metrics.

**Secondary:** Marketing professionals in SMEs who want credible,
peer-to-peer industry insight.

**Not our audience:** brand-new startups, enterprise corporates,
consumers.

---

## HUDL's social voice — the rules

1. **Lead with the insight, not the brand.** The first line should make
   someone stop scrolling because it's *true and they hadn't thought of it
   that way*, not because it mentions HUDL.

2. **Confident without being arrogant.** We know our stuff. We don't need
   to shout about it. State things plainly — let the quality speak.

3. **Commercially sharp.** Every post should connect to a business
   outcome. Not "here's a marketing tip" — "here's why this is costing
   you money."

4. **British English, no buzzwords.** Optimise not optimize. No
   "synergies", "disruptive", "game-changing", "unlock". If you'd be
   embarrassed to say it in a meeting, don't post it.

5. **Short paragraphs, one idea per line.** Never more than 2–3 sentences
   in a paragraph on LinkedIn. White space is working copy.

6. **End with a direction, not a demand.** "Worth checking before you
   scale any further." is better than "BOOK A FREE CONSULTATION NOW!!"

7. **No fabricated results.** Never claim "we saved Client X £50k" unless
   we have permission and a verified figure. Use "clients typically...",
   "in accounts we audit...", "we've seen ranges of..."

## Content pillars (rotate through these)

| Pillar | % of posts | What it looks like |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Commercial insight** | 40% | An observation about a marketing problem that costs businesses money. Ends with a quiet CTA. |
| **How-to / education** | 25% | A practical tip or framework. Short, actionable, no upsell. Builds authority. |
| **POV / opinion** | 20% | A take on an industry trend or common mistake. Slightly provocative but defensible. |
| **Behind the scenes** | 10% | What HUDL is working on, how we think, what we're seeing across client accounts. No client names without permission. |
| **Proof / social** | 5% | Client wins (anonymised or with permission), milestones, case study excerpts. |

## LinkedIn — the primary platform

**Format rules:**
- Hook line on its own (no full stop — leaves it open).
- 2–4 short paragraphs or a punchy list.
- One closing line that implies action.
- 3–5 hashtags, lowercase, at the very end. No hashtags mid-copy.
- Max 1,300 characters for algorithm reach; aim for 800–1,000.
- No images required for every post — strong text-only posts perform well.
- When using a visual: square (1080×1080) or landscape (1200×627).

**Hook patterns that work for HUDL:**

- Counterintuitive truth: *"Most Google Ads campaigns aren't optimised for profit. They're optimised for Google."*
- Cost framing: *"Running Google Ads without conversion tracking is paying for a billboard in the dark."*
- Direct challenge: *"Your agency's monthly report probably tells you nothing useful about your business."*
- Specific scenario: *"We audited 12 Google Ads accounts last quarter. 10 of them had the same problem."*

### LinkedIn example posts

---

**Example A — Commercial insight (PPC waste)**

Running Google Ads without conversion tracking is paying for a billboard in the dark.

You can see who drove past.
You can't see who came in.

Most SME accounts we audit are in this position — not through neglect, but because no one set it up properly when the account launched.

The algorithm then learns from clicks, not customers. Every pound you scale is amplifying a bad signal, not a good one.

Fix the tracking first. Then scale. In that order.

---

**Example B — POV / opinion (agencies)**

Most marketing agencies measure what's easy to measure, not what matters to your business.

Impressions. Click-through rate. "Reach."

None of those pay salaries.

The question worth asking your agency: "Show me how this activity connects to revenue."

If the answer is vague, that's useful information.

---

**Example C — How-to (budgeting)**

A simple framework for setting a marketing budget when you don't know where to start:

1. Pick one channel you already have evidence for (even if it's imperfect).
2. Calculate your current cost-per-lead on that channel.
3. Set a target cost-per-lead you'd be happy scaling at.
4. Work backwards from your lead volume goal.

You now have a number with a rationale — not a percentage of revenue pulled from thin air.

Start there. Adjust quarterly as the data comes in.

---

**Example D — Behind the scenes**

Something we see in almost every new client audit:

The campaigns that look expensive at first glance aren't always the problem.

Often it's one campaign, one ad group, one keyword — quietly burning 40% of the budget with a 0% conversion rate and nobody's flagged it because the account-level numbers look acceptable.

Zoom out and things look fine. Zoom in and there's a leak somewhere.

The audit is the work.

## X / Twitter — secondary platform

**Format rules:**
- Single tweet: ≤280 characters, punchy, standalone thought.
- Thread: hook tweet that ends "→" or "A thread:", then 4–7 numbered tweets, closing tweet with CTA.
- No hashtags mid-thread — one or two max at the end of the closing tweet.
- Tone: slightly more direct and wry than LinkedIn. Same voice, fewer words.

### X example posts

**Single tweets:**

> Most PPC agencies optimise for click-through rate. Your business optimises for profit. These are not the same objective.

> If you can't tell your agency which campaign generated your last 10 customers, you're flying blind. That's fixable.

> "We increased reach by 40%" is not a marketing result. It's a metric looking for a meaning.

> The removals industry spends more per click on Google Ads than almost any sector. The tracking to justify that spend is almost always missing.

**Thread opener example:**

> 5 things we find in almost every Google Ads account we audit →

Then: (1) conversion tracking firing on the wrong event, (2) broad match keywords eating budget, (3) search term reports unreviewed for months, (4) no negative keyword list, (5) campaign structure copied from a template and never questioned.

## Instagram — for when we build presence there

**Format rules:**
- Caption leads with a hook (first 125 characters matter before "more").
- Short lines, line breaks every 1–2 sentences.
- Emojis used once or twice maximum — functional, not decorative.
- Hashtag block at the end (15–20, mix of niche + broad).
- Story/Reel idea noted alongside every post.
- Visual always required — square (1080×1080) preferred.

**Content angle for Instagram:** Less B2B heavy than LinkedIn. More
behind-the-scenes, process, "day in the life of a marketing consultancy",
visual tip cards, short-form video commentary.

## What makes a strong visual for HUDL social

When Solo generates a Canva visual, it should follow these rules:

- **Dark background** — near-black (`#0c0c0c`) or pure black. Never white or pale.
- **Four-colour stripe accent** — yellow / blue / red / cyan bar (top or bottom).
- **Typography:** Barlow Condensed for headlines (uppercase, heavy weight); Source Sans 3 for body.
- **One strong statement** on the graphic — not a paragraph. Max 10 words.
- **Colour hierarchy:** yellow for the primary CTA/hook; cyan for supporting text; white for body.
- **Logo/wordmark:** HUDL.GG in the bottom-right corner, small.
- **Platform sizing:** 1080×1080 square for Instagram/LinkedIn; 1600×900 for X/Twitter header posts.
