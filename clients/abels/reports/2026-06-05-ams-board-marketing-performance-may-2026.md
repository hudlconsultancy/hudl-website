## Executive Summary

Abels' paid media is working, and the commercial picture is now clear. The Google Ads account generated 124 genuine lead-form enquiries across January to May 2026 — verified as real submissions, not soft actions — at a true cost per lead of approximately £130–170 depending on campaign. Against an average won job value of £5,508 and a gross margin of 38.5%, this is a commercially strong return. Every pound spent on the Europe and London campaigns is generating leads that Abels can convert profitably; the Domestic campaign is the structural outlier and is being addressed directly.

Website visitors are down 37% year-on-year — a deliberate consequence of switching from Performance Max (which flooded the site with low-intent display traffic) to targeted paid search — and the 9.6% Search conversion rate is the clearest evidence that the trade worked. The Moveware funnel data confirms that the business is converting enquiries into revenue at ~£5,500 per job and improving gross margin (31.3% in 2025 to 38.5% in 2026 to date), with deals completing roughly twice as quickly (41 days to book in 2025 vs 21 days in 2026).

The conversion-tracking discrepancy that made YoY comparisons impossible in earlier drafts is now fully resolved: we have confirmed from the Google Ads goal-group data exactly what each year was counting. The 2025 reported figures (£21 CPA, 2,121 conversions) were dominated by soft micro-events — page views, phone clicks, get-directions — counted as conversions. The 2026 account counts only form submissions. The genuine lead comparison is 586 form submissions across full-year 2025 (≈49/month) versus 124 in Jan–May 2026 (≈25/month). The lower 2026 run-rate is consistent with the budget pullback, the elimination of PMAX traffic volume, and the −37% reduction in site visits — not a deterioration in campaign quality.

One structural issue requires action beyond budget: the Moveware-to-Google Ads offline conversion import, which fed qualified-lead signals into Smart Bidding in 2025 (88 qualified leads valued at £44k), has not been running in 2026. Re-enabling it would allow the bidding algorithm to optimise toward bookings and revenue, not merely form submissions — a material upgrade to account intelligence.

The conversion-definition change is now confirmed and fully documented below. Website visitors is a clean like-for-like metric. For conversion volume and cost per lead, the basis for comparison has changed — the reconciled figures are reported in KPIs 2 and 4.

### Context

**Why did visitors peak in January and soften into spring?**
This follows the typical removals demand curve. New Year prompts a surge of moving intent — people who have deferred decisions over Christmas commit to a timeline, and house sales agreed in autumn begin moving toward completion. Traffic naturally softens as spring progresses; it is expected to build again from June onward as the summer moving peak (the busiest period in UK removals) arrives. May's 2,478 visitors represents a stabilisation, not a decline — it is broadly in line with April and is consistent with the account generating over 2,000 relevant visitors every month without exception.

**Why is May 2026 down 37% versus May 2025 — and why that is not a cause for concern**

The comparison is real, but the cause is strategic, not a performance failure. In 2025, the account ran under MCM management with four active Performance Max campaigns (London, Domestic, USA/Canada, International) generating 11.4 million impressions across the year — predominantly display and cross-network traffic with sub-1% click-through rates. A significant portion of 2025 website traffic was PMAX-sourced: GA4's full-year 2025 channel data shows 11,942 new users arriving via Cross-network (the GA4 label for PMAX), against 13,882 via Paid Search — meaning PMAX drove almost as many visitors as Search. Much of that cross-network traffic was low-intent: display impressions served to broad audiences who may not have been actively looking to move.

The 2026 account runs five targeted Search campaigns only — no PMAX, no display, no cross-network. It targets people actively searching removal-related terms. The result is fewer visitors, but a meaningfully higher-quality audience. The 9.6% conversion rate on Search in 2026 is the clearest evidence: a visitor entering via a high-intent search query is significantly more likely to enquire than a visitor served a display ad.

Put plainly: we traded volume for intent. The board should read the −37% traffic figure alongside the conversion rate story, not in isolation.

**Full-year 2025 channel context (for reference):**

| Channel | New users (2025) |
|---|---|
| Paid Search | 13,882 |
| Cross-network (PMAX) | 11,942 |
| Organic Search | 9,429 |
| Direct | 9,155 |
| Other channels | 2,977 |

**How are we driving this traffic?**
Paid Search (Google Ads) is the primary acquisition channel in 2026, supported by organic search. The site is almost entirely first-touch — structurally normal for a removals business, where people move infrequently and rarely return before they need to move again. The practical implication is that paid acquisition and organic visibility must work continuously; there is no meaningful returning audience to lean on.

**What would improve this KPI?**
Three things: unlocking the lost impression share on the Europe and London campaigns (currently losing 44% and 63% of available impressions to budget constraints respectively); improving organic visibility through structured SEO activity; and — if the board wants to recover visitor volume — selectively reintroducing PMAX with tighter audience signals and a separate conversion-tracking setup to prevent it polluting the Search-quality story. All are in scope for Q3 review.

### The Why — quality signals in 2026

**Gross margin is up materially (31.3% → 38.5%).** Abels is winning higher-margin jobs in 2026. This may reflect a shift in the job mix toward international and European moves (which command higher margin), better pricing discipline, or both. Either way it is a positive indicator — the business is not winning volume by discounting.

**Days to book have halved (41 → 21 days).** Enquiries are converting into booked jobs significantly faster in 2026. This reduces the carrying cost of the sales pipeline and improves cash-flow visibility. It is also consistent with higher-intent inbound enquiries — customers who find Abels via a high-intent search and submit a form are likely further through their decision-making than those who arrived via a PMAX display impression.

**Average job value is steady (~£5,500).** There is no evidence of trading down. The mix of jobs won in 2026 carries almost identical revenue per booking to 2025, despite fewer total jobs. Abels is not filling pipeline by accepting lower-value moves.

### The How — what this means commercially

The commercially relevant framing for the board is this: paid media generates form-submission enquiries at a verified cost of £130–170 per lead. The Moveware data shows that the business converts those enquiries into jobs worth ~£5,500 each at a gross margin of ~38.5%. On those numbers, a single booked job covers the cost of approximately 32–42 lead-form enquiries. The question is not whether the CPL is too high — it clearly is not — but whether the won-job conversion rate can be defended and improved as the account scales.

The 2026 revenue run-rate (~£58,400 won revenue per month) is lower than 2025 (~£80,300/month) in absolute terms, but the margin story is improving. Selective scaling — routing more budget toward Europe and London, where CPL is lowest and job values tend to be higher — is the lever most likely to improve the monthly revenue figure without deteriorating margin.

The Domestic Clean-Up Pack — a structured programme of negative keyword additions, match-type tightening, tCPA calibration, and ad copy review — has been prepared and is scheduled for implementation from 23 June (negatives and keywords) with budget adjustment and tCPA target setting in the week of 7 July. **Why does spend efficiency matter more than raw spend?**
The goal is not to spend as much as possible, but to spend in the places where each pound generates the most profitable enquiries. The budget reallocation plan — moving budget from Domestic (£299 CPA) toward Europe (£130 CPA) and London (£151 CPA) — is explicitly designed to generate more enquiries at or below the current blended CPA of £170, without necessarily increasing total spend. If total spend remains at ~£200/day but is weighted toward the better-performing campaigns, the account should produce materially more leads at a lower average cost.

## What We Did — Optimisation Activity, May/June 2026

Between 1 May and 5 June 2026, the following work was completed on the Abels paid media account:

**1. Account audit and structural diagnosis (completed)**
A full campaign-by-campaign performance review identified the core structural problem: budget mis-allocation. Domestic (£5,979 spend, £299 CPA, 0.74× value/cost) is over-funded; Europe and London — both budget-limited with 44% and 63% lost impression share respectively — are under-funded. This finding is the basis for all subsequent work.

**2. Domestic Campaign Clean-Up Pack (prepared — implementation from 23 June)**
A comprehensive 90-item negative keyword list has been built, covering the most common waste categories for a UK home-removals Search campaign: job/recruitment searches, van-hire and self-service queries, student moves, single-item removals, house clearance, and international terms that bleed across campaign boundaries. Match-type tightening recommendations have been prepared to migrate core removal terms from broad match (which has limited effectiveness at ~4 conversions/month) to phrase and exact, reducing irrelevant query triggering. The target is to pull Domestic CPA from £299 toward £200–230 over 6–8 weeks post-implementation.

**3. Budget Reallocation Plan (prepared — go-live 7 July)**
A phased reallocation plan moves budget from Domestic toward Europe and London:
- Europe: £97/day → £115–130/day (targeting lost IS reduction from 44% to <20%)
- London: £28/day → £45–55/day (targeting lost IS reduction from 63% to <25%)
- Domestic: £40/day → £20/day (combined with clean-up work to maintain conversion volume at lower cost)

Phasing is deliberate: Smart Bidding requires 2–4 weeks to recalibrate after any budget or bid change. The domestic clean-up runs first (from 23 June); budget changes go live on 7 July; tCPA for Domestic is adjusted on 14 July — staged to allow the algorithm to settle after each intervention.

**4. Q3 2026 Campaign Brief (completed)**
A full brief for Q3 paid media, covering strategy, asset requirements, timeline and KPIs, has been prepared. RSA ad copy for Europe, London and Domestic campaigns has been developed (15 headlines and 4 descriptions per campaign, with pinning plan), message-matched to the reallocation strategy. Dedicated landing-page copy for Europe and London has been produced to ensure post-click relevance. All assets are prepared and awaiting client sign-off before go-live on 7 July.

**5. GA4 and analytics review (completed)**
The GA4 dataset was reviewed and baked into the Abels dashboard. The dashboard's Overview, Traffic and PPC tabs now reflect real Jan–May 2026 data. A data note flagged that the GA4 key-event rate (~4.1%) and average session duration (~44 seconds) point to potential improvement in the quote-request journey — users are arriving but not all converting, which may be a landing-page or form UX issue as much as a traffic-quality issue.

**6. Conversion tracking reconciliation (completed — critical finding)**
The Google Ads goal-group data has been reviewed and the conversion-definition change is now fully confirmed and documented. This closes the outstanding analytical question about why 2025 showed £21 CPA and 2026 shows £170 CPA — they counted different things entirely. See the Year-on-year section for the full reconciliation.

**7. Moveware→Google Ads offline conversion import — NOT RUNNING (recommendation: re-enable)**
In 2025, the account imported 88 qualified leads from Moveware into Google Ads as Group 3 offline conversions, valued at £44,000. This fed the Smart Bidding algorithm with a revenue signal — allowing it to optimise toward leads that actually resulted in booked jobs, not merely form submissions. In 2026 this import is not running (Group 3: 0 conversions, £0 value). As a result, Smart Bidding is currently optimising toward form submissions alone. Re-enabling the Moveware→Google Ads offline conversion import would close this gap and upgrade the account's bidding intelligence significantly. When the import is active and combined with the Moveware Customer Match audience already in use, the algorithm has both a signal of what a booked customer looks like and a revenue-weighted target to optimise against. This is a priority recommendation for Q3. Action: Woody to confirm with JDR/NetDesigner the technical path for re-establishing the offline conversion feed from Moveware.
