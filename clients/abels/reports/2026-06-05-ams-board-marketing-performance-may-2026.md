## Executive Summary

Abels' paid media is working. The Google Ads account generated 97 enquiries at a blended cost of £170 per lead across the first five months of 2026 — a solid performance for a premium removals service, and well within profitable territory given the value of each removal job. Website traffic remains consistent, with May delivering 2,478 active visitors against a Jan–Apr average of 2,713 per month — a modest seasonal softening that is entirely normal as early-year moving intent cools into spring. The account's structural challenge is clear and already being addressed: budget has historically been over-concentrated in the Domestic campaign, which returns the weakest results, while the Europe and London campaigns — the most efficient performers — have been throttled by insufficient budget. The reallocation and clean-up work we have completed this month sets the platform for a materially stronger Q3.

## KPI 1 — Website Visitors

### The What

| Period | Active Users | Sessions |
|---|---|---|
| January 2026 | 3,474 | 3,943 |
| February 2026 | 2,728 | 3,161 |
| March 2026 | 2,414 | 2,814 |
| April 2026 | 2,235 | 2,576 |
| **May 2026** | **2,478** | **2,847** |
| Jan–Apr average | 2,713 | 3,124 |

**May 2026 vs Jan–Apr average:** -8.6% on active users; -8.9% on sessions.

The overall Jan–May 2026 total is 13,329 active users across 15,847 sessions.

### Context

**Why did visitors peak in January and soften into spring?**
This follows the typical removals demand curve. New Year prompts a surge of moving intent — people who have deferred decisions over Christmas commit to a timeline, and house sales agreed in autumn begin moving toward completion. Traffic naturally softens as spring progresses; it is expected to build again from June onward as the summer moving peak (the busiest period in UK removals) arrives. May's 2,478 visitors represents a stabilisation, not a decline — it is broadly in line with April and is consistent with the account generating over 2,000 relevant visitors every month without exception.

**How are we driving this traffic?**
Paid search (Google Ads) is the primary acquisition channel, supported by organic search. The site is almost entirely first-touch: of 12,633 visitors recorded Jan–May, approximately 12,404 were new users. This is structurally normal for a removals business — people move infrequently and rarely return to the site before they need to move again. The practical implication is that paid acquisition and organic visibility must work continuously; there is no meaningful returning audience to lean on.

**What would improve this KPI?**
Two things: unlocking the lost impression share on the Europe and London campaigns (currently losing 44% and 63% of available impressions to budget constraints respectively), and improving organic visibility through structured SEO activity. Both are in scope for Q3.

## KPI 2 — Conversion Volume

### The What

The Google Ads export covers the full period 1 January – 31 May 2026 as a single aggregate. It cannot be broken down by month from the data currently available.

**Jan–May 2026 aggregate (Search campaigns):**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Total conversions | 96.89 |
| Monthly implied average | ~19.4/month |
| Conversion rate | 9.60% |

This is a healthy conversion rate for paid search in a high-consideration, high-value service category. For context: average conversion rates across all industries on Google Search sit below 5%; a 9.6% blended rate indicates that Abels is attracting well-qualified, high-intent traffic — people actively searching for a professional removals company, not browsers or researchers.

**Note on conversion definition:** Google Ads records a "conversion" when a user completes a key action tracked on the Abels website (most likely a quote form submission or equivalent). This is a lead/enquiry, not a booked job. The relationship between conversions and actual revenue is addressed under Conversion Quality below.

### Context

**Why is the monthly average ~19 leads?**
The account is running at a constrained budget level across all five campaigns. The two best-performing campaigns — Europe (46.89 conversions, £130 CPA) and London (19 conversions, £151 CPA) — are both categorised as "budget limited" by Google, meaning they stop serving ads mid-day because their daily budgets are exhausted. This means the 97 conversions recorded Jan–May represent a floor, not a ceiling — there is demonstrable, quantified headroom. The budget reallocation work completed this month directly targets this.

**How are conversions generated?**
All five Search campaigns feed into the conversion count. Europe (Outbound) generated the most leads (47, or 48% of total volume) despite representing 37% of spend — the clearest evidence of where budget should be concentrated. London generated 19 conversions from the smallest absolute spend on a productive campaign.

## KPI 3 — Conversion Quality

### The What

Conversion quality — the percentage of enquiries that meet Abels' qualification criteria (e.g. right geography, right service, right move size, progresses to survey or quote) — is not measurable from Google Ads or GA4 data alone. This requires input from Moveware (the group CRM) to cross-reference enquiries against jobs surveyed, quoted and booked.

### Context

**Why does this matter?**
A campaign that generates 100 enquiries at £100 each is only better than a campaign generating 50 enquiries at £150 each if the quality of those leads is equivalent. For Abels — a premium service handling high-value domestic and international moves — a lower volume of well-qualified leads is preferable to a high volume of mis-matched enquiries. This is particularly relevant for the Domestic campaign, where the current £299 CPA and low conversion rate suggest a proportion of clicks and form fills may be coming from searches that are not well-matched to Abels' service tier.

**How will we improve our view of this KPI?**
The Moveware CRM export, cross-referenced against the Google Ads conversion data, will reveal which campaigns and keywords are producing enquiries that progress to survey. We have requested this data. In parallel, the domestic clean-up work (negative keywords, match-type tightening) is designed to reduce the volume of poorly-matched traffic before it reaches the quote form — improving lead quality upstream.

**Data required:** Moveware/sales team input on qualified lead rates per channel. See Data Required section.

## KPI 4 — Cost per Lead (CPL)

### The What

**Jan–May 2026 aggregate CPA by campaign (Search):**

| Campaign | Spend | Conversions | CPA | Performance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Europe (Outbound) | £6,119 | 46.89 | £130 | Best in account |
| London | £2,872 | 19.00 | £151 | Strong — most budget-constrained |
| Brand | £72 | 3.00 | £24 | Exceptional (brand defence) |
| International | £1,391 | 8.00 | £174 | Efficient — small sample |
| **Domestic** | **£5,979** | **20.00** | **£299** | **Requires attention** |
| **Account blended** | **£16,432** | **96.89** | **£170** | **Within target range** |

The blended £170 CPA is commercially sound for a premium removals business. A single domestic removal typically generates £1,000–£3,000+ in revenue depending on move size and distance; an international or European move considerably more. At these revenue levels, a £170 lead cost represents a strong return, subject to close rates — which is why Moveware data matters.

### Context

**Why is the Domestic CPA so much higher than the rest?**
The Domestic campaign is spending £300 per enquiry against a blended account average of £170 — and it is the only campaign returning less conversion value than it costs (0.74× value/cost, versus Europe at 2.57×). The diagnosis is query quality: the current keyword and match-type structure is triggering ads against searches that are not well-matched to Abels' service (for example, man-and-van queries, self-storage searches, or recruitment terms). This is a fixable structural issue, not a strategic one — Domestic removals are core Abels business and should remain a priority campaign.

**How are we fixing this?**
The Domestic Clean-Up Pack — a structured programme of negative keyword additions, match-type tightening, tCPA calibration, and ad copy review — has been prepared and is scheduled for implementation from 23 June (negatives and keywords) with budget adjustment and tCPA target setting in the week of 7 July. The target is to bring Domestic CPA from £299 toward £200–230 over the 6–8 weeks following go-live. Europe and London receive increased budgets from the same go-live date to recover lost impression share.

| **Total** | **£200/day** | **~£6,000/month** |

The implied monthly average spend (£3,286) is notably lower than the total daily budget allowance (~£6,000/month). This apparent underspend reflects a combination of factors: the aggregate is divided over five months including January (when spending may have begun mid-month or campaigns were still being optimised), and budget-limited campaigns automatically pause delivery when their daily cap is reached — they do not always spend their full daily budget, particularly if Quality Score issues limit ad rank. The actual monthly spend figures will be confirmed via a month-segmented export.

**Note on Performance Max and total account spend:** the Google Ads account total (all campaign types) shows £23,375 in the same period. The £16,432 referenced throughout this report covers Search campaigns only (the five JDR-managed campaigns). The remainder (£6,943) is Performance Max. All CPL and campaign-level analysis in this report covers Search.

### Context

**Why does spend efficiency matter more than raw spend?**
The goal is not to spend as much as possible, but to spend in the places where each pound generates the most profitable enquiries. The budget reallocation plan — moving budget from Domestic (£299 CPA) toward Europe (£130 CPA) and London (£151 CPA) — is explicitly designed to generate more enquiries at or below the current blended CPA of £170, without necessarily increasing total spend. If total spend remains at ~£200/day but is weighted toward the better-performing campaigns, the account should produce materially more leads at a lower average cost.

**Confirmed budget ceiling:** the client needs to confirm whether the budget ceiling is £200/day (constrained, ~£6,000/month) or £245/day (unconstrained, ~£7,350/month). The reallocation plan covers both scenarios. A confirmed figure is required before go-live.

## What We Did — Optimisation Activity, May/June 2026

Between 1 May and 5 June 2026, the following work was completed on the Abels paid media account:

**1. Account audit and structural diagnosis (completed)**
A full campaign-by-campaign performance review identified the core structural problem: budget mis-allocation. Domestic (£5,979 spend, £299 CPA, 0.74× value/cost) is over-funded; Europe and London — both budget-limited with 44% and 63% lost impression share respectively — are under-funded. This finding is the basis for all subsequent work.

**2. Domestic Campaign Clean-Up Pack (prepared — implementation from 23 June)**
A comprehensive 90-item negative keyword list has been built, covering the most common waste categories for a UK home-removals Search campaign: job/recruitment searches, van-hire and self-service queries, student moves, single-item removals, house clearance, and international terms that bleed across campaign boundaries. Match-type tightening recommendations have been prepared to migrate core removal terms from broad match (which has limited effectiveness at ~4 conversions/month) to phrase and exact, reducing irrelevant query triggering. The target is to pull Domestic CPA from £299 toward £200–230 over 6–8 weeks post-implementation.

**3. Budget Reallocation Plan (prepared — go-live 7 July)**
A phased reallocation plan moves budget from Domestic toward Europe and London:
- Europe: £97/day → £115–130/day (targeting lost IS reduction from 44% to <20%)
- London: £28/day → £45–55/day (targeting lost IS reduction from 63% to <25%)
- Domestic: £40/day → £20/day (combined with clean-up work to maintain conversion volume at lower cost)

Phasing is deliberate: Smart Bidding requires 2–4 weeks to recalibrate after any budget or bid change. The domestic clean-up runs first (from 23 June); budget changes go live on 7 July; tCPA for Domestic is adjusted on 14 July — staged to allow the algorithm to settle after each intervention.

**4. Q3 2026 Campaign Brief (completed)**
A full brief for Q3 paid media, covering strategy, asset requirements, timeline and KPIs, has been prepared. RSA ad copy for Europe, London and Domestic campaigns has been developed (15 headlines and 4 descriptions per campaign, with pinning plan), message-matched to the reallocation strategy. Dedicated landing-page copy for Europe and London has been produced to ensure post-click relevance. All assets are prepared and awaiting client sign-off before go-live on 7 July.

**5. GA4 and analytics review (completed)**
The GA4 dataset was reviewed and baked into the Abels dashboard. The dashboard's Overview, Traffic and PPC tabs now reflect real Jan–May 2026 data. A data note flagged that the GA4 key-event rate (~4.1%) and average session duration (~44 seconds) point to potential improvement in the quote-request journey — users are arriving but not all converting, which may be a landing-page or form UX issue as much as a traffic-quality issue.
