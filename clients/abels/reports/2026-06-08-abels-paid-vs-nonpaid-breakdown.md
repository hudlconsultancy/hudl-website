## May 2026 vs May 2025 (YoY)

**Paid (Google Ads)**
| | May 2025 | May 2026 | YoY |
|---|--:|--:|:--:|
| Spend | £3,027 | £5,929 | +96% |
| Clicks | 1,981 | 467 | −76% |
| Avg CPC | £1.53 | £12.70 | — |
| Conversions | 123 (soft actions incl.) | 37 (genuine lead forms) | not like-for-like |

*Why clicks fell while spend rose: in 2025, paid spend included cheap Performance Max/display clicks and counted soft actions as conversions. In 2026 we pay for fewer, genuine, qualified search clicks — hence higher CPC but real enquiries.*

*Organic visibility fell but quality rose sharply — average ranking improved ~17 places (page 4+ → page 2–3) and click-through more than doubled. The step-change occurred around Sept–Oct 2025 (website rebuild / cleaner indexing).*

---

## What it tells us

1. **Organic carries the volume** — 65% of search clicks at £0 media cost — but it's largely brand/navigational, and the commercial pages still rank page 2–3. That's the SEO growth lever.
2. **Paid is now lean and intentional** — far fewer, far more expensive clicks (£1.53 → £12.70) because we stopped buying cheap low-intent traffic; every paid click now works toward a genuine enquiry.
3. **The measurement gap** — we can attribute paid enquiries (124–136) but **not organic/direct enquiries**. Supplying the GA4 channel-split export would let us report leads — not just clicks — by channel, and finally show paid vs organic *conversion* contribution.
