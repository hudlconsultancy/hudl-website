## What it shows (like-for-like, same period)

- **More leads, fewer jobs:** lead volume is **up 18% (669 → 788)** but jobs won are **down 28% (76 → 55)** and won revenue is **down 24% (£388.6k → £296.9k)**. The lead→opportunity rate fell from 40% to 28%.
- **Driven by a lead-mix shift, not collapsing demand:** in 2026 the **"Abels Main Webform" alone contributed 204 leads but converted at just 2% to a won job** (18% to opportunity). A large volume of low-converting webform leads is inflating the lead count without producing jobs — it dilutes the headline conversion rate.
- **Profitability per job improved sharply:** **gross margin rose 26.5% → 38.4%**, total won margin is actually **up 11% (£102.8k → £113.9k)** despite lower revenue, average job value is up 6%, and **average time-to-book halved (51 → 21 days)**.
- **Net read:** the business is winning **fewer but better, faster, higher-margin jobs** on a noisier top-of-funnel. The revenue dip is real and worth watching; the margin and speed gains are genuinely positive. Some 2026 leads may still be in-pipeline, but the 21-day average book time means most that will convert already have.

**Caveats:** all-source (not paid-media attributable); won figures may firm slightly as the most recent leads mature; "Abels Main Webform" surge in 2026 warrants a quality/qualification check (lead capture change?).
